## Recovery decision

The config entry anchors the established installation. A companion-owned, readback-verified history Store reserves revisions before snapshot writes and preserves retired publishers. Partial writes fence operation; restart validates history and snapshot together. The current journal head must exceed all retired publisher maxima. Recovery with intact history requires a revision strictly above both all reserved journal revisions and any validated retained snapshot, plus the exact confirmation-state precondition. Damaged/lost history, including a validated snapshot publisher that conflicts with the journal publisher, requires a coherent full HA backup and subsequent app reconciliation. Such a partial restore can erase replacement identities; all normal and recovery writes are fenced even at a higher revision, with degraded `storage_invalid` status and no storage reset. This deliberately chooses safety over accepting a guessed reset. History supports 32 identities without eviction. The only profile remains inert `default`.

The production error behavior is stricter than the historical spike: any persistence failure is uncertain until restart reconciliation, because history may already reserve that revision. A healthy status means the validated snapshot matches verified storage/history; dispatch is still false and playback has not been verified. See the [operator guide](../companion/README.md) for installation, upgrade, backup, replacement and recovery procedures.

## Evidence boundaries

All data and credentials are synthetic. The probe starts no management app and proves recovered snapshots survive app absence and actual HA process restart. No compatibility outside HA 2026.10.2 is claimed. Power loss, physical filesystem/controller caches, disk exhaustion, hardware fault behavior, backup UI and Supervisor/HA OS or household restore are unverified. They remain blockers to stronger deployment-specific durability guarantees, not claims inferred from SIGKILL. Full loss/rollback of config and all stores is fundamentally indistinguishable from new installation locally; the future publisher's startup/reconnect and M2 restore guard remain mandatory. If journal history cannot be recovered coherently, this release safely refuses recovery rather than fabricating revision history.
